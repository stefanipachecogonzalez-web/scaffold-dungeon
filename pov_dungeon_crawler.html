Goals, Outcomes & Objectives

When planning an English lesson, teachers begin with a goal — the broad purpose of learning. From this, they develop learning outcomes: what students should be able to do by the end of a unit or course (e.g. Students will be able to ask for and give directions). From outcomes, teachers create specific learning objectives for individual lessons (e.g. Students will use imperatives and prepositions of place to give directions).

Scaffolding

Once teachers have the objective, they identify the support students need — called scaffolding. Scaffolding is temporary assistance (from a teacher, peer, or resource) that helps learners perform tasks they could not complete independently. Examples: visual aids, graphic organizers, model texts, sentence starters, guided questions, demonstrations. As students gain competence, scaffolds are gradually removed so learners perform independently.

Materials & Constructive Alignment

Materials are selected because they help learners move toward the intended outcome — not simply because they are engaging. For a lesson on giving directions: maps, model conversations, role-play cards, visual vocabulary guides. This reflects constructive alignment: goals, outcomes, objectives, scaffolding, materials, and assessment all working together.
